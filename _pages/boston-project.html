Boston: population density
and employment geography

By Jing Gan

Population: ACS 2020–2024 (5-year estimates)
Employment: LODES 2023 (primary jobs)

Does the choice of city center change how we understand population density? This project makes the effects of center definitions, study boundaries, and spatial scale visible.

Center choice matters less than study extent

In the 20-mile sample, three reference centers yield similar population density gradients. Enlarging the study area from 10 to 20 miles produces a more noticeable change: the fitted decline becomes less steep.

14.3–14.6%

Fitted decline in population density per additional mile across City Hall, the employment-weighted centroid, and the strongest employment peak in the 20-mile sample.

19.4% → 14.6%

Fitted decline per mile using City Hall, when the sample radius expands from 10 to 20 miles.

A stable reference point does not imply a unique boundary

Employment data define a reference peak independently of the population regression. A second set of experiments holds cells at 250 meters and changes the smoothing bandwidth. The area common to three tested bandwidths contains 653,618 primary jobs across 28.375 km² of grid area, including water.

Professional services, health care, education, and finance all contribute to this employment cluster. Agreement across specifications provides sensitivity evidence; it does not establish statistical confidence or an official central business district. In this experiment, the intersection coincides with the smallest-bandwidth candidate.

Data and analytical approach

  1. Combine ACS population estimates with TIGER tract land areas to calculate population density.
  2. Aggregate LODES primary jobs to projected grids and use Gaussian smoothing to identify employment concentrations.
  3. Compare City Hall, an employment-weighted centroid, and the strongest employment peak; examine alternative study radii, grids, bandwidths, and thresholds.
  4. Use unsmoothed industry job counts to interpret the resulting employment areas.

The baseline population sample contains 742 tracts within 20 miles of City Hall; 731 enter positive-density log regressions. Distances are straight-line distances. Regressions are descriptive and do not incorporate ACS sampling uncertainty or adjust inference for spatial dependence. Population and employment refer to different periods.
